VNFairy Creator Guide

Welcome to VNFairy Designer

VNFairy is a visual design suite for creating stunning, branch-based visual novels running on modern HTML5 standards. You don't need any coding experience: the Designer handles your storyline structure, assets integration, and settings, and generates a packaged, standalone game.

Tip: Use the light and dark mode control in the top-right header to suit your workspace lighting.

Understanding the Workspace Layout

The visual designer is split into three main areas for browsing the project, writing the story, and checking the result:

1
Left Sidebar (Scenes & Assets):

Browse scenes, groups, and collapsible asset categories at a glance. Use the compact Manage button in either tab to open the larger workspace for searching, organising, and bulk changes.

2
Center Workspace:

Switch between Dialogue for Cards or Script writing, Story map for routes, Unlockables for gallery content, and Settings for novel-wide presentation and variables.

3
Right Panel (Stage Preview & Inspector):

Preview the active line and configure its voice, formatting, and character placements. The preview follows the novel's light or dark theme, including the dialogue box and readable text colours.

Asset & Scene Management

The sidebars keep everyday browsing compact. Open their Manage buttons whenever you need a larger, searchable workspace or want to change several items together.

Asset Management

The asset workspace separates Backgrounds, Characters, Music, Voice & sound, and Videos into tabs. You can search by name, tag, or type; filter by folder or favourites; sort the library; inspect usage and unused files; preview media; and replace an asset everywhere it is referenced.

  • Choose an asset type and use Bulk upload to add several files together.
  • Select visible assets to move them to a virtual folder, add tags, or delete them in one action.
  • Create configured characters from the Characters tab with New character.

Scene Management

Use the Scenes tab to search by name, ID, or tag and filter by status or group. Selected scenes can be moved to a group, assigned Draft, Review, or Final status, or deleted together. Each scene row also provides Open, Settings, Duplicate, and Delete actions.

Use the Groups tab to create and maintain the sections used to organise the story.

Scene Settings & Modes

Visual novels are split into individual Scenes. Select a scene and choose Scene settings above the Dialogue workspace to open its tabbed settings modal. Basics, Media, and Behaviour are separated so related controls stay easy to find.

  • Basics: Display name, status, group, tags, and continuity or revision notes.
  • Media: Scene mode, background, music, dialogue font, and mode-specific video or text settings.
  • Behaviour: Endings, transitions, character dimming, bookmarks, and player menu behaviour.

Scene Modes

Each scene has a primary layout template called a Scene Mode:

  • Normal Dialogue: Standard gameplay with character portrait sprites, background graphics, and a bottom dialogue box.
  • Video Cutscene: Automatically plays a selected cutscene video file. Includes a built-in skip button for the player.
  • Fullscreen Text Cutscene: Displays dialogue text centered on screen over your selected background, perfect for narrator-only prologues or transitions.
  • Auto Text Cutscene: Centered fullscreen text that automatically advances to the next dialogue step after a pause.
  • Image Cutscene: Renders a background image with optional auto-progress duration. Great for splash cards, locations, or CG illustrations.

Writing Dialogue & Formatting

The Dialogue workspace has two views. Cards provides a visual line-by-line editor and inspector, while Script keeps longer scenes compact and makes bulk editing easier.

Cards View

Choose + Add Dialogue Line to add a card. Clicking a card opens its properties in the Inspector on the right, where you can set voice, formatting, and character placement.

Timeline Navigation Shortcuts

To speed up visual novel writing, the editor supports the following timeline shortcut interactions:

  • Click a dialogue card to select it and update properties.
  • Double-click inside the dialogue textarea to edit content directly.
  • Use the delete icon on the card header to remove steps.
  • Use the duplicate control to copy a step while preserving character placement and speaker settings.
  • Drag dialogue cards up or down to re-order your narration sequence.

Script View & Bulk Editing

Switch to Script or press Alt+S to work with a denser list of lines. Each speaker field opens the searchable character chooser and includes Narrator. Use Configure beside the keyboard hints to assign up to nine speakers to Alt+1 through Alt+9. Use the bookmark and delete icons on individual rows, or select several lines to:

  • Set one speaker for the whole selection using the same searchable chooser.
  • Duplicate the selected block.
  • Move the block with the up and down arrow buttons.

The toolbar also provides story-wide Search & replace and reusable scene, dialogue-layout, and character pose presets.

Text Styling & Typewriter FX

VNFairy renders dialogue on-screen using a smooth typewriter animation. Under the Inspector's Text Formatting section, you can format selected lines:

  • Bold: Renders text in high weight.
  • Italic: Slants characters, great for thoughts, internal monologues, or sound effects.
  • Underline: Adds lines under text to emphasize terms or speaker emphasis.
💡 Formatting: You can apply multiple formats (e.g. bold and italic) simultaneously by checking both options.

Characters & Expressions

Portraits are the primary visual driver of visual novels. VNFairy supports multi-expression characters that enter, exit, and adjust their position on the stage.

1. Creating a Character

Before putting a character on the stage, you must define them. Open the Assets sidebar tab, choose Manage, then use New character on the Characters tab. In the Creator modal:

  1. Input a Character Name (displayed in dialogue boxes).
  2. Select a custom Dialogue Name Text Color (used in-game to identify active speakers).
  3. Define Expression Sprites (e.g., happy, sad, shocked). Upload a distinct image file for each state.
  4. Set the default character scaling slider (from 50% up to 200%) to fit sprite resolutions.
⚠️ Non-Visual Characters: Check "Non-visual character" for off-stage or telephone/intercom voices. They will display dialogue boxes but won't look for sprite resources or occupy stage placement slots.

2. Stage Placement Slots

Each dialogue step can place characters in three distinct slots:

  • Left Slot: Aligns sprite to the left 25% boundary.
  • Center Slot: Centers sprite on screen.
  • Right Slot: Aligns sprite to the right 75% boundary.

In the Inspector, select the character and their specific expression for each slot. The active speaker slot will automatically be highlighted, while other slots dim to guide the reader's attention (dimming can be globally toggled in Scene Settings).

Audio: BGM, Sound, & Voice Clips

Enhance the immersion of your visual novel with music tracks, sound effects, and character vocal performances.

Background Music (BGM)

Music is configured at the Scene Level. In Scene Settings, select a loaded BGM file. The engine transitions to this track upon entering the scene. Selecting Continue Current BGM allows seamless transitions between connected scenes without audio gaps. To silence music, select Stop Music.

Voice Clips & SFX

Audio cues can be triggered on individual dialogue lines. In the Dialogue Step Inspector, look for Voice Clip / Sound Effect:

  • Select an uploaded audio file.
  • The engine automatically plays this sound the exact moment the typewriter dialogue step begins rendering.
  • Perfect for voice acting clips (per line) or ambient noises (e.g., doors opening, phones ringing, birds chirping).
🔊 In-Game Options: Players can adjust Music and SFX volume levels separately via the in-game Settings panel, which automatically saves their preferences.

Branching, Variables & Logic

Branching creates the interactive pathing that makes visual novels compelling. Manage it at the bottom of the Dialogue workspace under Scene Advancement & Branching.

Linear vs. Option Choices

  • Linear: Choose "Go to Next Scene" to direct the player to a target scene when they click past the final dialogue line.
  • Show Choices (Branching): Prompts players with a list of interactive choices. Add as many choices as you want. Each choice routes to a different scene path.

Project Variables

Variables hold boolean flags, numbers, short text, or simple inventories. First, define them in the Novel Settings tab under Project Variables (e.g., romance_lily, gold, has_key).

Adjust variables during options. In the Choice editor, configure modifications:

  • Use the visual action builder to set, increment, decrement, or toggle a value. Inventory actions can add, remove, or set an item count.

Conditional Scene Jumps

In linear advancement mode, expand the collapsible Conditional Scene Jumps section to add overrides. Each rule checks a variable before routing to its destination; when no rule matches, the normal next scene is used.

Example: "If romance_lily >= 10, jump to lily_good_ending, else jump to neutral_ending."

Choice Unlock Criteria

Each choice card keeps its destination, Requirements, and Actions grouped clearly. Add multiple requirements to control when the choice is available and multiple actions to update flags, numbers, text, or inventory when it is selected. A choice without requirements is always available; a choice without actions only routes to its destination.

Unlockables & Gallery Settings

VNFairy features built-in support for a Gallery on the main title screen. When enabled, players can unlock and view backgrounds, character sprites, and play music tracks after playing through them in the game.

Configuring Gallery Content

Go to the Unlockables tab in the Center Panel to setup settings:

  1. Enable/disable the global Gallery feature and toggle individual sections (Characters, Backgrounds, and Music BGM).
  2. Click + Add Unlockable Asset to add a gallery registry card.
  3. Select the target asset file (from your uploaded backgrounds, characters, or BGM).
  4. Input a display title and brief description (e.g., "Unlocked by finishing Chapter 1").

Unlocking Triggers

Define what triggers the unlock in the Gallery registry cards. Choose from three unlock conditions:

  • Reaching a Scene: Unlocks as soon as the player enters a target scene.
  • Obtaining a Route Ending: Unlocks when a route ending scene is reached.
  • Checking a Tracked Variable: Unlocks if a variable meets a value (e.g., affection >= 20).

Customizing Title & Credits

Control the branding, presentation, and concluding roll of your visual novel. Configure these global settings under the Settings tab.

Title Screen Styling

Customize the visual layout of the opening splash screen:

  • Title Background & Music: Choose a background image and track to play when opening the novel.
  • Menu Layout: Choose menu positions: Left sidebar, Centered column, Right sidebar, Bottom horizontal row, or Horizontal Coloured Bar.
  • Menu Button Style:
    • Blur (Glassmorphism): Elegant semi-transparent frosted backing.
    • Solid color: Opaque button block colors.
    • None (Transparent): Clean text layout over the background image.
  • Border & Text Colors: Tailor accent border colors and text colors.
  • Title Text Toggle: Check "Hide Title & Author Text" if your background image already has the game title logo pre-rendered.

Ending Credits Roll

Configure the scroll sequence that rolls when route endings are reached:

  1. Set the background image and closing music BGM.
  2. Input credits text in the credits text editor. Markdown formatting is fully supported!
  3. Use linebreaks to space out team roles, asset credits, or special thanks.

Preview, Debugging & Exporting

Once your story is orchestrated, test its branching logic and package it for distribution.

Preview & Playtest Debug Panel

Click Preview in the header to run your novel in an interactive sandbox. If the "Test Play Debug Menu" is enabled in settings, a red Debug button appears in the top-right corner of the player. Opening this panel allows you to:

  • Jump to Scene: Search and warp instantly to any scene in your game.
  • Active Variables Tracker: View real-time values of all numeric variables.
  • Unlock All: Instantly unlock all Gallery items and achievements to verify completion rate designs.

Packaging Your Game

Click Export game in the header toolbar to compile your project into a self-contained ZIP archive. VNFairy exports:
1. All media assets in their respective subfolders.
2. The playback engine structure (HTML, CSS, JS).
3. An offline fallback loading system.

Offline play: Exported games include JavaScript scene wrappers for direct local play. If you are opening an older or manually assembled project and the browser blocks local JSON, drag-and-drop its project.json onto the player or run it from a local web server.
